PROCEDURE -- work through these steps in order before you answer.

Step 1. Re-read the question, then the rubric. Write down (to yourself) what a 5 would
        contain for this specific question.
Step 2. Check relevance first. If the answer addresses a different question, relevance is
        low no matter how good the content is.
Step 3. Check correctness: is anything stated here actually false? A confident false claim
        caps correctness at 2.
Step 4. Look for first-hand evidence -- numbers, tool names, decisions they made, things
        that went wrong. Textbook phrasing with none of that caps practical_experience at 2,
        even when the content is correct.
Step 5. Score depth on whether they explained a mechanism or only named it.
Step 6. Score communication on structure, not on length or confidence.
Step 7. List what the rubric expected and they did not say. That list is `missing`, and it
        is what the follow-up must target.
Step 8. Pick the verdict from the scores you just assigned, not from how the answer felt.
Step 9. Set `information_coverage`: walk the rubric point by point, count how many are now
        satisfied by EVERYTHING said on this question so far, and divide by the total. If a
        follow-up only restated an earlier point, the number does not move.
Step 10. Choose the next action -- the shape a follow-up would take if one is asked.
Step 11. Write the follow-up as one spoken sentence that quotes their words, targets the
        rubric points still missing, and reveals nothing about the score.

PROCEDURE -- work through these steps in order before you answer.

Step 1. Read the rubric first, before the answer. Fix in your mind what a 5 would contain.
Step 2. Read the answer once for what it CLAIMS, and note whether it answers the question
        that was asked or a nearby easier one. That decides relevance.
Step 3. Hunt for evidence, and count it: numbers, tool names, version details, a decision
        they made, something that went wrong, a before-and-after. Write the count down.
        Zero pieces of evidence means practical_experience is 1 or 2, no matter how
        confident or well written the answer is. This is the step most often skipped.
Step 4. Check each factual claim against what you know. One confident false statement caps
        correctness at 2. Do not average a false claim away against true ones.
Step 5. Ask whether they explained WHY or only named WHAT. Naming a tool or restating the
        question in other words is depth 1-2. Explaining a mechanism or a trade-off is 4-5.
Step 6. Score communication on structure alone. Do not let fluent writing raise the other
        four scores -- that is the single most common judging error.
Step 7. Compare your five numbers against the rubric one final time. If every number is 4,
        you have almost certainly not discriminated; go back to Step 3.
Step 8. Pick the verdict that matches the numbers you assigned, not the impression the
        answer left.
Step 9. Write `reasoning` as one sentence naming the ONE thing that decided the score.
